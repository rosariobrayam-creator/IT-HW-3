Q2: Why Arngren.net Is a Bad Website

The site I chose is Arngren.net, a Norwegian online store that sells gadgets and electronics. It is still live, and it keeps showing up in bad web design examples published in 2026. Here are 10 reasons it is a bad website.

  1. Information overload. Huge numbers of products are crammed onto one page with no clear structure, so visitors don’t know where to start.
  2. Too many images. Product pictures are everywhere and compete for attention, so nothing stands out.
  3. No visual hierarchy. Headings, deals, and categories all have about the same weight, so you can’t tell what matters most.
  4. Inconsistent sizes and spacing. Images and text blocks are different sizes and placed haphazardly, which makes the page hard to scan.
  5. Tiny, hard-to-read text. Small type packed tightly together makes reading product details a chore.
  6. Confusing navigation. It’s hard to see where one category ends and the next begins, so finding one product means scrolling through everything.
  7. Search and filters aren’t prominent. Shoppers can’t easily narrow their choices, so they have to inspect thumbnail after thumbnail.
  8. Poor mobile experience. The layout isn’t designed to adapt to small screens, and usability studies of the site point out weak mobile responsiveness.
  9. Accessibility problems. One published WAVE accessibility check of the site reported 192 errors and 312 contrast errors, which makes it hard for people using assistive technology.
  10. Looks outdated and untrustworthy. The dated look and endless scrolling can make shoppers hesitate to trust the site with their payment details.
