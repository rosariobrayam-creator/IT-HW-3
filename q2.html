Q2: Why Craigslist Is a Bad Website

The site I chose is Craigslist, a classified ads website that launched in 1996 and is still used in hundreds of cities. A 2026 review still describes its interface as outdated. Here are 10 reasons it is a bad website, based on how it looks and how its pages are laid out.

  1. It looks decades old. The site has not been redesigned since it launched and looks outdated.
  2. It is almost all plain text. The home page is mostly text links with very few graphics, so nothing guides your eye.
  3. Links and buttons don’t look clickable. Even buttons are shown as plain text, so it isn’t obvious what you can click.
  4. No visual hierarchy. Categories and long lists of subcategories look about the same, so you have to read every one to find what you want.
  5. Plain colors with little contrast. A mostly white page with default blue links and it makes hard to tell the difference between each one.
  6. Inconsistent styling. Some navigation items are black while the clickable links are blue, so similar elements look different.
  7. Categories aren’t visually distinct. Housing, gigs, and for sale have no colors, icons, or pictures to tell them apart. Icons only appear on a few actions like reply and flag.
  8. No clear navigation bar. Main tasks like posting and searching aren’t easy to spot, which makes exploring the site a chore.
  9. Unfamiliar to newer users. The early-2000s style is a hurdle for younger users who expect modern layouts and buttons.
  10. The dated look hurts trust. The old-web look can feel suspicious, and one usability tester wondered how much help they would get if something went wrong on a site that puts so little effort into its design. A 2026 review also points to limited safeguards.
